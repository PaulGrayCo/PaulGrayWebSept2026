## 5. Methodology (PASS and IP)

**PASS** = Performance Aquatics Swimming System. Repo: `PaulGrayCo/PerformanceAquaticsSwimmingSystem` (MkDocs Material, Cloudflare Pages behind Cloudflare Access). Last substantively updated mid-2026; credential content is stale.

**Differentiator:** the technical and scientific layer (CSS, USRPT, periodisation, biomechanics) delivered through the human layer (trust, connection, discipline). The human layer is what makes the science land.

### Coaching framework (the coach-community IP)

- **Environment before technique.**
- Within environment: **Discipline → Connection → Trust.**
    - Discipline and structure: black and white rules; "easier to loosen the rope than tighten it".
    - Connection: comforts and discomforts, likes and dislikes, receptiveness to instruction.
    - Trust (the linchpin): diagnose → adapt communication → build. Builds slowly, breaks fast.
- Research link: Mageau & Vallerand (2003) info pack maps autonomy support, structure and involvement onto this hierarchy (`docs/methodology/research/mageau-vallerand-2003.md`).

### Swimmer development pipeline (August, naming open)

Assess → Build Foundations → Develop Technique → Apply Under Load → Compete and Review. In-water only for now. Standard toolkit: cue library, drill bank, assessment sheets, progress trackers. Monthly snapshot and seasonal review for parents. Sub-framework name idea: *Streamline* (undecided).

### Training methodology

- Unified approach: keep USRPT's specificity principle, restore what pure USRPT drops (aerobic base, resistance, mobility, power). Three-tab widget built (weekly schedule, components, phase shifts).
- Testing: CSS from 400/200 (run 400 first; fatigue skews results), plus 50/25 for speed-endurance gap.
- Reference: Cam McEvoy low-volume sprint model. Considering a monthly trial of his "The Lab" tier for coaching content.
