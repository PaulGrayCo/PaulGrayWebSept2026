Swimmers to be inspired by

The international competitive swimming landscape features an exceptional generation of athletes routinely shattering world records and sweeping major titles. These swimmers represent the pinnacle of modern swimming across sprint, distance, and medley disciplines.

Elite Men

🇫🇷
Léon Marchand
France

The undisputed king of the individual medley and breaststroke. Back-to-back World Aquatics Male Swimmer of the Year, Marchand holds the legendary 400m IM world record and dominates multiple disciplines under elite pacing.

🇨🇳
Pan Zhanle
China

The fastest man in water, holding the mind-boggling 100m freestyle long-course world record at a blazing 46.40 seconds.

🇿🇦
Pieter Coetze
South Africa

The rising backstroke superstar who captured the overall title at the 2026 World Aquatics Swimming World Cup tour, proving his short-course and long-course mastery over the world's best.

🇭🇺
Kristóf Milák
Hungary

A butterfly virtuoso who continues to put on a clinic in the 100m and 200m fly. He holds the record for the most sub-51-second long-course 100m butterfly performances in history.

🇭🇺
Hubert Kós
Hungary

A lethal backstroke and individual medley technician training in the US collegiate system, routinely breaking short-course metres (SCM) records in the 100 and 200 backstroke.

🇦🇺
Cameron McEvoy
Australia

The veteran speed-demon who revolutionised ultra-short-distance training, remaining a dominant force in the 50m freestyle world rankings and recently breaking the long-standing world record.

🇨🇦
Josh Liendo
Canada

A dominant force in sprint fly and freestyle. Liendo holds the short-course 100m butterfly world record (47.68) and electrified the international stage with a blistering 50.42 Commonwealth Games gold in Glasgow.

🇺🇸
Ilya Kharun
USA

Having recently transitioned from Canada to Team USA, this butterfly phenom continuously rewrites history, lowering the SCM 50m butterfly U.S. Open and American record down to a stunning 21.41 seconds.

🇰🇾
Jordan Crooks
Cayman Islands

One of the most explosive sprinters the NCAA and short-course circuits have ever seen. After a brief hiatus, Crooks returned for Tennessee and took down Hubert Kós's U.S. Open record in the 50m backstroke (22.60).

🇺🇸
Carson Foster
USA

The ultimate tactical ironman in the individual medleys and mid-distance freestyle. Foster is a consistent world-class podium fixture, regularly challenging the likes of Marchand in the gruelling 400m IM.

🇷🇺
Egor Kornev
Russia

A rising backstroke talent making waves on the international circuit with exceptional technical precision and emerging as a serious contender in both 100m and 200m backstroke events.

🇬🇧
Adam Ramsay-Peaty
Great Britain

The legendary breaststroke king—who added the surname Ramsay following his marriage—launched a 20-month hiatus comeback. At 31, the multi-time Olympic champion took bronze in Glasgow and is targeting LA 2028.

Elite Women

🇨🇦
Summer McIntosh
Canada

A generational prodigy and dominant multi-event weapon. McIntosh rules the women's landscape across the 200m/400m individual medleys and butterfly events, regularly trading Female Swimmer of the Year honours.

🇺🇸
Kate Douglass
USA

A versatile Swiss Army knife of swimming who recently rewrote history by smashing the 50m freestyle world record (23.19). She handles world-class times in everything from sprint freestyle to the 200m breaststroke.

🇳🇱
Marrit Steenbergen
Netherlands

Fresh off securing the overall 2026 Swimming World Cup women's title, she is a dominant powerhouse in the 100m freestyle, holding a new short-course world record of 50.52 seconds.

🇺🇸
Gretchen Walsh
USA

The supreme underwater and butterfly technician who consistently pushes sprint boundaries, driving teammates and competitors to world-record times.

🇦🇺
Kaylee McKeown
Australia

An absolute titan of backstroke. McKeown holds the rare distinction of simultaneously controlling the 50m, 100m, and 200m backstroke world records while expanding her range into elite breaststroke and medley events.

🇺🇸
Katie Ledecky
USA

The greatest distance swimmer of all time. Ledecky continues to rewrite longevity records, maintaining an iron grip on the 800m and 1500m freestyle events globally.
